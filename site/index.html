VERDICT

The browser uses an approximate normal quantile; the tool computes it exactly. The false-accept rates on the benchmark page are measured, not estimated.

The contract is one line of clauses

    Every clause is key=value. The comma is the whole grammar. A quoted value may contain commas; a semicolon separates a list; a hash starts a comment; a newline is whitespace, so the contract may be wrapped after any comma and still parse to exactly the same contract.

    One command, three exit codes

    0 proven · 1 not proven or conflicting · 2 bad input. The gate is a CI step without a wrapper script.

    Not another dashboard. A verdict.

    Eval harnesses report means and confidence intervals but do not correct for the fact that the candidate was selected on the very set they report. HELD reads the frozen control and decides.

    ⌾

    A gate, not a graph

    The verdict comes from the held-out interval alone. A candidate passes only when the lower end of that interval clears the declared minimum gain. Everything else is reported, nothing is skipped.

    ≈

    The winner's curse, corrected

    The gain on the selection set is inflated by about one standard error times the expected maximum of the candidates produced. HELD reports the inflation and what remains after removing it.

    ⚑

    The conflict, named

    A candidate that looks better on the selection set but does not clear the held-out bar is reported as conflicting, not as unproven. That is the exact shape of a loop that has fooled itself.

    ⌗

    How much evidence

    held plan computes how many held-out items a declared gain needs at a declared significance and power. Proving a one point gain on a binary metric needs about 39 000 paired items.

    ⌸

    A pinned protocol

    The SHA-256 of the canonical contract is the pin. A claim cannot be re-described after the fact without breaking the pin, and held seal adds the hash of the data file.

    Four languages, one source

    English, Russian, Spanish and Chinese render from the same contract, so a number cannot drift between translations. A safety or review protocol in the reader's language, from one file.

    The contract

    One line, one file, one hash. It declares what was measured, on what, with what threshold, and it is the thing you publish.

    Clauses

    The scores file is one record per outcome: a split, a system, an item and a score. JSON Lines or a JSON array. Nothing else is required, so any harness can emit it.

    One contract, four languages

    Real output of held render on the same contract and the same scores. The numbers are identical because there is only one number.

    Measured, not asserted

    2 000 rounds per configuration on a synthetic benchmark with a known truth. Every acceptance rule sees the same rounds. The nominal error rate is 5 %.

    Selection items Candidates Greedy Significance on the selection set HELD
    Method. A round produces N candidates; the loop keeps the one that scores best on the selection split; the winner is then measured on the held-out split, which took no part in the selection. The truth is known by construction, so the false-accept rate is measured rather than argued about. The generator is a SplitMix64 stream with an explicit seed, so the numbers are reproducible byte for byte on any machine. The harness and the shipped gate were driven over the same rounds and disagreed on 0 of 201 rounds.
    What this is not. The benchmark is synthetic. It reproduces the mechanism of the selection bias, not the published numbers of any particular paper. HELD pays for its honesty in power: with 1 000 held-out items it will refuse to certify a five point gain, because a thousand items cannot prove five points. held plan tells you what would.

    Questions, answered plainly

    Does it replace my evaluation harness?

    No. It reads outcomes. Your harness produces a scores file, one record per outcome, and HELD decides whether the difference between the two systems survives. It does not run models, agents or robots, and it does not need to know what produced the scores.

    Is the held-out set really never used for selection?

    HELD cannot know that. It takes the split labels as a claim from the owner of the data. What it can do is pin the contract and the data file, so a reviewer can see that the protocol did not change between rounds. That pin is the reason the receipt exists.

    Why does it refuse improvements that other tools accept?

    Because it will not certify what the data cannot support. On the benchmark, greedy acceptance wrongly keeps a candidate with no real improvement in 89.9 % of rounds and the held-out rule in 2.3 %, against a nominal 5 %. The price is power, and held plan states the price in items.

    Does this work for robot policies?

    Yes. A robot policy improvement is a success rate over tasks, and the arithmetic is identical: a selection set of tasks used to pick the policy, a held-out suite never used for selection, and a candidate that wins on the first and ties on the second. The published work on recursive self-improvement in robotics reports exactly this failure: changes kept passing their own tests while the target task never succeeded.

    Is the correction exact?

    It is an upper bound. The prediction assumes the candidates in a round are independent. When they share a baseline, their errors move together, the real inflation is smaller and the correction is conservative. The benchmark measures both, and the corr clause lets you model the correlation explicitly.

    Does it need the network?

    No. One static binary, no runtime dependencies, no account, no telemetry, no network. Everything it does is arithmetic on files you already have.
